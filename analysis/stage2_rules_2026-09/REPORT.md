Nothing live was touched: no `route`/`screen`, no claims, no spend; `filter/spec/` is unchanged

## How it was measured

`filter.engine route` cannot use a scratch store (it registers the release in Postgres), and
`diagnose` skips the overlay and alias map. `route_scratch.py` runs `route`'s input path and
writes each rule's matches per work to `cache/stage2_rules_2026-09/`; `variants.py` derives the
piles. It reproduces c048 for all 5.1M works except 10 alias pairs (9,106 vs 9,105 in
`screen_expensive`). Postgres read-only. ≈13k free Crossref/doi.org lookups cached under
`cache/doi_registry/`.

## Step 8 — `curated-observatory`

- Matches 2,394 works; 1,924 are in `screen_expensive` today.
- **Shadowed, 579 still get in** (title-broad 445, title-strong 98, cited-title 33,
  osf-registration-completed 3); **1,345 are lost** — real replications: the screen proceeded
  1,229, 908 are in `data/extracted.csv` (~1,100 rows), only 3 lack an abstract. Every live
  admission rule reads the title only; these papers state their design in the abstract.
- **Shadowing it alone is unsafe:** `extract/export.py` renders only works the release admits,
  so those 908 works would leave `extracted.csv` at the next release.
- No general rule recovers most of them without roughly tripling the pile ("new" = beyond
  today's 9,105):

| Candidate | Lost recovered (/1,345) | New works | Known replications per 1k new |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Proposed `replication-claim-general` | 533 (507 screen-proceed) | 21,622 | 29.9 |
| `replication-claim-text` (shadow today) | 861 | 45,841 | 16.3 |
| `replication-claim-text` + `-residual` | 991 | 76,376 | 10.7 |

  At ~$161 per 100k screened, the proposed rule's 21.6k new works cost ≈$35 to screen
  (extraction of what proceeds not measured).
- The proposed rule: three high-yield abstract arms from `replication-claim-text`; two title
  shapes (subtitle "replication", "replication of the/a…") minus molecular-biology senses;
  "we … replicated" / "replication study" only when the text also mentions an original/prior study.
- **Recommendation:** keep curated live. Apply `01-add-shadow-specs.patch` (both new specs as
  shadow) at the next deliberate route. Hold `02-curated-shadow-general-live.patch`: even with
  the general rule live, 523 extracted works would leave the export.

## Step 9 — DOI twins (issue #210)

7,435 of the 9,105 admitted works have a comparable DOI (covering every extracted work).

- **No twins beyond the known 30.** 27 of them have a sibling pool record with the real title.
  All are admitted only through `curated-observatory`.
- **A second fault: 8 real replications carry an unrelated paper's DOI** (7 with
  extracted/set-aside rows). Discarding would lose real studies, so the rule excludes them;
  they need a Stage 3 guard. Two withdrawn PsyArXiv records also mismatch (W4400060759 is in
  `extracted.csv`).
- **Threshold:** all 30 twins score 0.0, all 7,285 matching works ≥ 0.867 — any cut in
  0.15–0.85 is equivalent; 0.5 used. Compares against registry subtitle, original-language and
  short titles; a different script counts as a translation unless year or a sibling says
  otherwise; registration date is a year only when no publication date exists. No FLoRA or
  extracted work is flagged.
- **Pool-wide, without Crossref:** 12,538 DOIs held by more than one record have a record that
  shares no title with its siblings (~12.5k likely twins), but only 69 of those are admitted.
- **Rule data:** a rule cannot call Crossref at route time, and a twin shares its DOI with the
  real paper, so a new engine primitive `work_id_in` matches OpenAlex record ids (committed; no
  live spec uses it). `doi-registry-twin` (shadow discard, precedence 950) is generated by
  `doi_twins_audit.py --fetch --spec`, like `aliases.json`; the engine evaluates it to exactly
  the 30. Durable version (designed, not built): a frozen Crossref data file as a release input,
  threshold in the spec.
