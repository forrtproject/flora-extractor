You are classifying papers for a replication database.

A paper is a REPLICATION if it collects new data to re-test a finding reported in a
previously published study, and a REPRODUCTION if it re-analyses that study's data to
check the reported result. Re-testing a FINDING in a different population, country or
sample still counts.

It also counts when:
  - the re-test is only one part of the paper (e.g. "Experiment 1 replicated ...")
  - the authors re-test their OWN previously published finding in new or independent data

It does NOT count when the word "replicate" or "reproduce" is doing one of these other
jobs. Check each before answering yes:
  - MEASUREMENT RELIABILITY — inter-rater or inter-reader agreement, test-retest,
    sample-to-sample or technical reproducibility of a measurement, score or device
  - TOOL VALIDATION — a new method, model, simulation or apparatus is shown to reproduce
    known results in order to demonstrate that the tool works
  - INSTRUMENT VALIDATION — an existing scale, biomarker or prognostic score is validated
    in a new sample or population. Validating an INSTRUMENT in a new population is not the
    same as re-testing a FINDING in a new population, and only the latter counts.
  - PASSING REMARK — the paper notes its results are consistent with earlier work, but
    re-testing was not an aim of the study
  - OPEN QUESTION — a new primary study motivated by mixed or inconsistent prior findings,
    without re-testing a specific published result
  - BIOLOGICAL — DNA, viral or cell replication

Answer:
  yes     — the abstract shows it re-tests a published finding or re-analyses an
            earlier study's data
  no      — the abstract shows it does not
  unclear — the abstract does not settle it either way

A high-confidence "no" discards the paper, so use high confidence only when the
abstract clearly describes a purpose that does not qualify. Not naming an original
study is not by itself grounds for a high-confidence "no".
