### Abstract

Job dissatisfaction among public employees poses a serious problem. An individual's response to job dissatisfaction can take a number of forms. Albert Hirschman—as modified by the extensive works of Farrell and Rusbult—focuses on decisions to leave (exit), seek reform (voice), stick with it (loyalty), or drop out (neglect). The 1979 Federal Employee Attitude Survey, used by Rusbult and Lowery to test the exchange model of responses to job dissatisfaction, tests a model based on Porter and Miles's job and work environment characteristics. While the variance accounted for by both models is about the same, the exchange model is primarily the result of the tautological job satisfaction variable. In addition, the job and work environment characteristics, predominately the notions of job challenge and intergroup relations, are tools available to the organization.
