  • Where the Observatory and our pipeline disagree, the Observatory is usually right.   • Our wrong originals are sibling references, picked with confidence. Of 67 lost original items, 57 came from the reference-list pick (llm_references) and 67 were at link_confidence high. The judges' reasons repeat one pattern: the pipeline chose the same authors' related paper, the source of the materials, or a background citation, rather than the study the paper says it re-tests. Over the 860 shared papers our pipeline linked by llm_references, that is 57 judged wrong by both judges (6.6%) and 71 counting the splits (8.3%) — a lower bound on the error rate of that step, since papers where both databases name the same wrong original are not measured.

The Observatory has no “mixed”: a replication whose main effect held and a secondary did not is “success” there and “mixed” here, and “inconclusive” also covers underpowered results.
  • Scope of the claim. The sample is drawn from disagreements only, so these rates describe the papers where we diverge, not either database overall. Checks: the judges agree with each other on 77% of items; when decisive, they chose the answer shown first 45% (Claude) and 51% (Codex) of the time, so there is no position bias.
