Where FLoRA's scope and the Metascience Observatory's part company

265 works the Observatory records as replications and our screen rejects — with the reason, for each one.

What was asked. 573 Observatory replications that our Stage 1 search gate never admitted were put to the shipped two-voter screen, unchanged (classify_replication(), deepseek-v4-flash + gpt-5.4-mini). It said proceed on 308 and discard on 265.

Read the rate carefully. Every work here is one the gate missed, and the gate looks for a replication word in the title or abstract. So this population is, by construction, papers that do not announce themselves — not a random sample, and not FLoRA's general false-positive rate.

The difference in one line. FLoRA asks what a paper says it is doing: a replication states an aim to check a specific earlier finding. The Observatory asks what a study does: any design that re-tests a reported effect counts, whether or not the authors frame it that way. Neither is wrong; the gap below follows from the definitions.
