Stage-2 Rule Book v2 — a whitelist in seven rules

Twenty specs become seven, covering replication only; reproduction moves to its own issue. Two whole precedence bands are deleted, not rewritten. The design is settled by measurement on local data and stress-tested against Gemini 3.1 Pro and codex. Companion to rulebook_review.html (what is wrong with the current book) and current_state_v4.html (the pipeline as a whole).

The short version. The exclusion band and the rescue band exist only because the admission signal is loose. Invert the architecture from blacklist ("a replication stem is a candidate unless an exclusion kills it") to whitelist ("nothing is screened unless a positive rule admits it") and both bands become unnecessary in the same move — no sense-disambiguation rules, no rescues, no ninety duplicated regexes, no manual sync obligation. What remains is four discards of two clearly different kinds, one strong admission rule, one weak admission rule, and an audited pending reservoir.

The measurement that decides the shape: six phrase arms cover 92% of known replication studies, and the other twenty-six arms in the current book buy 1%. One of those six — bare replication of — fires on 82% of screened negatives. Split the book there and everything else follows.

Scope: this book admits replications. The reproduction vocabulary (reproduce, re-analyse, computational reproducibility, CODECHECK) is a different genre with a different precision profile and needs its own rule, developed against its own evidence — issue #155, and the existing phrase-reproduction rule carries on unchanged in the meantime so nothing goes dark.

1 · The evidence this design rests on

All figures below were computed locally today against two corpora already in the repo. Method and biases are stated first, because the numbers are only worth what the denominators are worth — the failure that produced the 2026-08-04 reset (#151).

The positives

2,895 distinct replication studies with a usable abstract, from data/FLoRA entry sheet - replication list.csv (3,851 rows, 2,978 distinct doi_r, 1,550 carrying a validation_status). Text used is ref_r (an APA citation, which contains the title) concatenated with abstract_r — a close proxy for the pool row's title + abstract, not an exact one.

Bias, stated up front. A substantial share of the entry sheet was discovered by keyword search, so any phrase rule scored against it over-estimates recall, and over-estimates it most for the arms that did the finding. The sheet's columns do not let curated-external-list entries be separated from pipeline-found ones, so this cannot be corrected here — it has to be handled by the holdout design in §6. Read the recall figures as an upper bound and, more usefully, as a ranking of arms against each other, which the bias distorts far less.

The negatives

501 rows from data/not_a_replication.csv — papers that passed the old filter, reached the two-voter screen, and were judged not_a_replication.

Bias, stated up front. These are not a random sample of OpenAlex. They are a sample of what the old loose filter admitted and the screen then rejected — i.e. the false-positive load itself. So a figure like "5.8% of negatives" is not a population false-positive rate and must never be quoted as rule B's precision. What this corpus does measure, and measures exactly, is the composition of the money we are currently wasting — which is the question the rule book has to answer.

Bare replication of is the false-positive load. It is simultaneously the highest-recall arm in the book (40% of positives, 342 of them uniquely) and the source of four-fifths of the rows the screen throws away. Every exclusion rule in the current 500s band — DNA replication, replication forks, database replication, technical objects — is downstream of this one arm. The exclusions are a patch on a single loose regex.

It cannot be repaired with an anchor

The obvious fix is to require an object after "replication of". Three anchors were tested on the rows where replication of fires and no STRONG arm does:

The best anchor (proper name + year) is roughly break-even; the union admits three negatives per positive. The existing phrase-with-cite rule does no better: on bare-only rows its row-scoped author-year citation is present in 61% of positives and 42% of negatives. No cheap syntactic anchor makes bare replication of precise. It belongs in the cheap tier or in shadow, sized against the pool before it is switched on — and it must not be a route to the expensive screen, which is exactly what phrase-with-cite makes it today.

2 · The architecture: invert, then delete

The current book inherits a blacklist from the API-harvest era, when the search query was the admission decision: everything carrying a replication stem was a candidate, and rules existed to kill the wrong senses. That architecture produces a cascade — an exclusion for "DNA replication", then a rescue for a genuine replication that mentions a plasmid, then a copy of the exclusion's regex inside the rescue, then a precedence inversion where the rescue (650) outranks the only admission route (350) and quietly demotes real papers out of the two-voter screen.

Under a whitelist, none of that machinery has anything to do:

what the blacklist neededwhat the whitelist does instead
6 vocabulary-exclusion rules (biological, biological-of, structural, technical-object, technical-verb, data-availability) Deleted. "DNA replication" is not discarded — it is simply never admitted, because it matches no admission arm.
2 rescue rules (exclusion-rescue 173 lines, title-phrase-rescue 156) Deleted. Nothing to rescue from. The one job worth keeping — "a strong admission should beat a fallible discard" — becomes a precedence number (§3).
The 32-arm alternation written four times, hand-synced Written once, in rule B. The other three copies had no consumer once the exclusions and rescues are gone. (One caveat survives: §7.)
nfd-stems, a second stem rule for decomposed Unicode Deleted. NFC-normalise title and abstract once where the pool row is built; every downstream consumer gets it free.
The pyre_regex / RE2 divergence that keeps two rules in draft Moot. Both rules needing a proximity lookaround are exclusions.

The one thing the inversion does not buy, and both reviewers said it loudly: pending is now where recall goes to die. Codex: "a never-sampled pending pile is a silent discard pile with more comforting terminology." A whitelist is only defensible as a whitelist plus a monitored rejection reservoir. §6 makes pending sampling a release obligation, not optional maintenance — if that part is dropped, do not adopt this design.

3 · The seven rules

Precedence bands are re-cut. The 600s rescue band and the 500s exclusion band disappear; a new 700s admission band sits between the two kinds of discard, which is the whole trick.

The rescue band's job, done by ordering. Gemini's strongest objection to the strawman was that a structural discard can delete a real replication filed under the wrong work type, and that admission should therefore override it. That is right — but only for the fallible discards. Splitting the discards by failure mode and putting admission between them gets the protection with a precedence number instead of a 173-line rescue rule that re-implements everything it rescues from.

Two kinds of discard, two kinds of evidence. This is the split that makes the discards auditable. A rule at 940+ says this object cannot be a coded FLoRA record — either it is definitionally not the study, or it carries no text to code from. That claim is about the object, and it is settled by inspection. A rule at 500 says this registry type is not a study — a claim about a metadata crosswalk that can simply be wrong, which is why admission outranks it and why it owes a sampled precision estimate. Merging them, as the current book does at 945–960, hides the difference.

A1 · not-a-paper-doi discard 960

Two arms, kept as separately-labelled clauses so each can be audited and retired on its own evidence (codex was firm on this: merging rule files is fine, merging arm provenance is not — one overall 99.9% figure can hide one unsafe arm).

  • doi_regex: /reviews/|/decisions/ — a publisher-minted path segment for the review object. Recommend anchoring to known registrants rather than matching anywhere in the DOI.
  • doi_regex: \.suppl$ — a DOI ending in .suppl is the supplementary object minted alongside an article, not the article. Terminal-anchored, so it cannot match a DOI that merely contains the string. This is the identifier-side counterpart of the supplementary-materials work type in A3a, and the two overlap deliberately: different fields, either one alone is enough.
  • doi_prefix — the ten confirmed deposit-only registrants from today's rule. Change: drop the figshare 10.6084 arm. figshare is a general-purpose registrant hosting preprints and technical reports, not a deposit-only one; it is in the current rule as a regex purely because non_article_doi() matches it on a prefix boundary, which is an implementation detail leaking into policy.

The deposit arm rests on a claim that has never been measured: "the paper is in the corpus under its own DOI, so discarding the deposit loses nothing." Codex is right that this is a joint probability (parent DOI resolvable × parent in pool × parent admitted by some other route), not a structural fact. It stays live — the prior is strong and the volume matters — but §7/V1 makes measuring parent recovery a named obligation, and the answer can move this arm to a linkage worklist instead of a discard (#157, deferred).

A2 · not-a-paper-title discard 955

Today's editorial-artifact pattern, unchanged:

^\s*(?:review for|decision letter for|peer review #|
      faculty opinions recommendation of|correction to|author response)\b

Every alternative names a document genre followed by its parent, and the pattern is start-anchored, so it fires on titles that announce what the object is and not on papers that discuss the genre. A bare ^editorial arm would break that property — an article titled "Editorial independence and replication" is a paper — so no such arm is added.

Scope decision owed (D2 below): codex notes that correction to and author response objects sometimes contain a substantive failed reproduction. Both are already in the live pattern; whether they should be is a definition question for FLoRA, not a fact about the rule.

A3a · no-codable-text discard 940

type ∈ {dataset, software, supplementary-materials, component, database}.

The claim here is not "this is not a replication". It is that these objects carry no abstract and no prose, so even when one is the artifact of a replication there is nothing to screen and nothing to code an outcome from. That is a scope decision about what FLoRA can produce, and stating it that way is what makes it defensible — a deposit record is not being asserted to be off-topic, it is being asserted to be uncodable.

Two consequences follow, and both are better handled here than papered over:

  • It sits above admission at 940. A deposit titled "Replication Data for: …" will fire admission arms; being admitted would only buy a screen with no text to read, which the #149 pilot showed proceeds at ~70% precisely because the model cannot discard what it cannot read. Deleting dataset's free discard was the most expensive item in the current book, and this ruling reverses it on a rationale that does not need a precision sample.
  • The residual risk both reviewers raised is real and unchanged: where a deposit is the only record of a replication, that replication is lost. The mitigation would be a linkage worklist rather than an admission — record the deposit's parent DOI, check whether the parent is in the pool, and route the unlinked remainder to a human. That is deferred: #157. Deposit→parent resolution is not a metadata lookup — DataCite relation fields are unevenly populated and often point at a preprint or a sibling deposit, one deposit can serve several papers, and title matching fails precisely where the deposit's title and the article's title share nothing. What is not deferred is V1's parent-recovery measurement (§7), which sizes the loss for A1's deposit prefixes and A3a together and tells us whether the worklist is worth building at all.

component and database are in this list on the same argument, and are the two worth a second look — a component can occasionally be an independently meaningful report.

A3b · not-a-study-type discard 500

type ∈ {peer-review, grant, paratext, libguides, standard}. The remainder of today's list: registry types for objects that are affirmatively not studies and would have text if they were. Because the only thing standing between one of these and deletion is a metadata crosswalk, admission at 700 outranks it — a real replication mistyped as paratext gets screened instead of deleted.

letter stays out of both lists. Major journals publish short replication reports as letters, and a letter has an abstract. It is screened like any other paper. editorial and erratum are not in today's rule either; adding them is a decision, not a cleanup, so they are not added here.

Does B need "failed" and "successful"?

Measured marginally — i.e. what each adds once the arms above it are already in:

candidate armpositives it addsnegatives it addsverdict
failed replication(s) · failure(s) to replicate · unsuccessful replication(s)+1+0Add. A distinct construction the verb arm cannot reach, at zero measured cost.

So: yes to both, and all four arms go in. With them B reaches 82.6% of positives at 6.8% of the false-positive load, against 80.8% / 6.2% without.

Read the "+1" carefully — it is the clearest example on this page of why a marginal count is not a coverage argument. The noun phrase and the verb phrase are different constructions and neither reaches the other. "We present a failed replication of Smith (2009)" fires the noun arm and nothing else in B — not fail* to replicate, not we … replicat(e|ed), because "replication" there is a noun and the sentence never uses the verb.

The arm scores +1 marginally because in this corpus the 98 papers phrasing it that way almost all also say "replication study" or "we replicated" somewhere in the same abstract — and that corpus was assembled partly by keyword search, so its abstracts are systematically richer in the vocabulary that found them. A marginal count of +1 on a biased corpus is weak evidence of redundancy; a marginal count of +0 negatives is strong evidence of harmlessness. When an arm is free, the second number decides.

Codex's refinement, which I would adopt for the arms that report someone else's result: replication study, failed to replicate, direct replication and the many-labs family are strong in a title but can be literature-review language in an abstract. Split each into a title_regex arm at full strength and a text_regex arm requiring a current-paper cue (this|the present|our|we) nearby. That is a narrowing inside one rule, which is exactly the property the whole redesign is buying.

Deliberately excluded from B: bare replication of (§1.2) and registered report of (most Registered Reports are original studies) — both go to C or the probe rule. All reproduction vocabulary is out of scope for this book entirely (§3.7).

C · replication-signal screen_cheap 300

Enough signal to be worth one small discard-only model, not enough for two voters. Arms kept separately labelled so each can be sized and switched independently:

  • multilingual title stems (title-stem today — 111 of 112 Korean 재검증 works are invisible to the English stems, and this arm alone rescues 3.4% of known positives that no phrase arm reaches);
  • English replication stem in the title;
  • OpenAlex replication concept ids, conditional on an article-like type and a non-empty abstract;
  • bare replication of — last, and only after sizing. On current evidence this arm alone may be larger than the rest of the cheap tier combined.

D · replication-probe shadow 100

The genuinely new content in this design, and the reason it is not simply a smaller version of the old book. Both reviewers independently produced the same list of replication genres that carry no replication vocabulary at all — and no amount of tuning the word "replicate" will ever reach them:

  • "Revisiting X", "X reconsidered", "A new look at X", "On the robustness of X" — heavily used in economics;
  • "we repeated the experiment/study", "an independent test of", "external / out-of-sample validation of", re-examin*;
  • many-analyst projects, adversarial collaborations and forecasting tournaments whose abstracts describe the consortium, not a replication;
  • pre-2010 papers written before the vocabulary stabilised — "we attempted to confirm the finding";
  • non-English equivalents of verification / repeat experiment outside the current stem list.

Each is a separate shadow arm with its own id, promoted only on its own count and its own sample. This rule is where the whitelist's blind spot is made visible instead of silent.

The reproduction-side probes — re-estimat*, reimplement*, rerun, CODECHECK certificates and artifact-evaluation reports — belong to the reproduction rule, not here.

3.7 · Reproduction is carved out

Reproduction is a different genre with a different failure profile, and folding it into a replication rule book means the two are promoted and measured together when their evidence has nothing to do with each other. Bare reproduction of is animal breeding, social reproduction and epidemiological R₀ (8% precision over 15,440 rows, 0 of 15 sampled hits in our sense — #137); re-analysis of is dominated by secondary analyses asking new questions of old data; generic comput* reproduc* is workflow engineering, pedagogy and policy. Each of those needs its own sample, and none of them is settled by anything measured on this page — my positives corpus contains too few reproductions to say anything (comput* reproduc* fires on 4 of 2,895; anchored reproduction of the … results on 3).

So: no reproduction vocabulary enters rules B, C or D. Today's phrase-reproduction — ten anchored patterns, the best-argued rule in the current bundle — is carried into the new bundle verbatim, live, at its current precedence, so the reproduction stream does not go dark while its replacement is developed. Everything else about reproduction, including the two axes Stage 3 already codes it on, goes to #155.

5 · The no-abstract problem

Roughly a fifth of the pool has no abstract, and §1.3 says such a row loses about half its signal. The fix is a cascade, not a pile: a narrow title rule selects who is worth the effort, backfill tries to get the text, and a human or an LLM is spent only on what backfill could not recover.

narrow title rule            unmistakable replication titles only:
   │                         "A Direct Replication of …", "Registered Replication
   │                         Report of …", "Failure to Replicate …"
   ▼
abstract backfill            search/fetch_abstracts.py waterfall:
   │                         OpenAlex → EPMC → S2 → CrossRef → Scopus
   ├── recovered ──────────► normal routing: the row now has text, and rules
   │                         B / C decide it like any other row
   ▼
   not recovered ──────────► needs_human  (title + metadata review)

Three properties make this better than admitting title-only rows to a screen:

  1. The narrow rule is a budget gate, not an admission. It decides who gets backfill effort. It never sends a row to an LLM on a title alone — codex's objection holds: a two-voter screen given only a title mostly converts missing evidence into a paid "unclear".
  2. Backfill success returns the row to the ordinary path. No parallel evaluation, no title-only screening prompt to validate separately. Most rows should leave the cascade here.
  3. Only genuine dead ends cost human attention, and by construction they are rows whose titles are unmistakable — which is exactly the population where a human decides quickly from metadata.

Two supporting rules:

  • pending/no_text is the backfill worklist. The engine already emits the reason code and the waterfall already exists; they are not currently wired together. Priority order for the budget: known FLoRA DOIs → narrow-title matches → rule-C title stems with an article-like type → concept-only rows. Do not backfill 5M rows.
  • Never let a concept-only, no-abstract row reach a screen — the #149 finding, that unreadable rows proceed at inflated rates because the model has nothing to discard on. This is also why A3a discards the typeless-text objects outright rather than sending them round this cascade.

And report recall separately for abstract-present vs abstract-absent, by year, language and field. A measured small loss is acceptable; an unmeasured, systematically biased one is not.

6 · Implementation, in the order that pays

The organising principle: the expensive screen is the budget, so the first live rule should be the one that fills it (B), not the ones that shrink the pool (A). Discards only save money where they overlap what would otherwise be screened — and under a whitelist that overlap is small. Shrinking 5M to 3.5M is worth nothing on its own; putting 20k high-precision rows in front of two voters is worth everything.

Phase 0 — size everything before switching anything on (no LLM spend)

Author the new rules with every arm shadow and separately labelled, mint a release, and run route + sizing + diagnose over the pool. What comes back is the thing this entire design is currently missing: per-arm exclusive and overlapping counts on the real 5M-row distribution, plus abstract-missing rate, type composition, prefix composition, and overlap with known FLoRA DOIs. Estimated screen cost falls straight out.

Blocker to clear first: the pool is not in this checkout (cache/snapshot_pool/ is absent, cache/engine/ is empty). Pull it via search.pool_sync before Phase 0 — and, per the 2026-08-04 incident note, verify row counts against the dashboard before anything reads it.

Phase 1 — rule B live, capped (first real spend)

Turn on only the title-position and first-person arms of B → screen_expensive, in deterministic capped batches. This produces the first high-yield worklist and — more valuable — the first downstream precision estimate, since the screen's own verdicts on B's rows are a measurement that costs nothing extra. Everything else stays shadow.

Phase 2 — audit, then the discards live in order of confidence

Sample before switching on, per subarm (§7/V1). A1 and A2 first (definitional). A3a next — its rationale is a scope claim rather than a precision claim, so what its audit has to establish is narrower: that the types really do lack codable text, and how often a deposit is the only record of its replication. A3b last, since it is the one whose failure mode is a wrong crosswalk. None of this needs to precede rule B operationally — only the overlap rows cost money.

Phase 3 — widen B one family at a time

Add abstract-position arms with current-paper cues, then completed multi-lab reports. After each addition measure the marginal count and marginal precision — gross per-arm precision over overlapping hits will flatter a weak arm hiding behind a strong one. Read a small marginal count as "cheap", not as "redundant": §3 B shows how a distinct construction can score +1 on this corpus and still be the only arm that reaches a real phrasing.

Phase 4 — C live through the cheap tier, arm by arm

Order: multilingual title stems → English title stems → concept + article-type + abstract → bare replication of only if Phase 0's sizing and a sample justify it. Keep the cheap tier's discards non-operative until that tier's recall is validated on this distribution (§7/V4).

Phase 5 — pending audit becomes a standing obligation

Every release samples pending, or the whitelist has no way to learn what it is missing. Promote probe arms out of D on their counts.

7 · Validation plan

The rule that killed the last one: LLM output is not a reference label for a rule, nor for the screen evaluating that rule. Human labels are the authority; LLMs may triage and flag disagreements. Before any sampling, write a short adjudication manual distinguishing completed replication · completed computational reproduction · protocol or planned replication · robustness/secondary analysis · internal replication cohort · discussion of replication · artifact linked to a replication · unrelated homograph. Two trained coders label independently, blind to the selecting rule where feasible; a third adjudicates; 10–15% of agreements are re-audited.

V0 — freeze the holdout first

filter/spec/holdout.json has been referenced in three docs and never created; diagnose reports not_constructed for all 20 rules. Sequester a random 20–30% of FLoRA doi_r, stratified by year, field and language, before any rule is authored. Design on the remainder. Do not look at the misses until the book is frozen. Without this, the first promotion tunes on its own test set.

V1 — discard precision, per subarm (A1 · A2 · A3a · A3b)

~300 independently labelled hits per subarm with zero errors supports a 95% lower bound near 99%; ~600 for 99.5%; a 99.9% claim needs ~3,000 — and "trusted" prose is not a substitute for any of them. Review all hits for small arms. Stratify DOI prefixes by prefix and period. Sample every work type separately and oversample software, component, database, supplementary-materials.

Threshold: autonomous discard requires ≥99.5% estimated precision with a one-sided 95% lower bound ≥99%, and no confirmed eligible study unless its parent is demonstrably present and admitted by another route. Any subarm with one genuine unrecovered study goes back to shadow, or is referred to the deferred linkage route (#157).

Plus the deposit obligation: among "Replication Data for X" records, measure the proportion with a resolvable parent DOI × parent present in the pool × parent admitted — this is the measurement #157 is blocked on, and it runs whether or not the worklist is ever built. The discard argument is that joint probability.

V2 — rule B admission precision

Precision here means "the expensive screen should reasonably look at this", not "this reaches Stage 3". Stratify by arm, field, title-vs-abstract match, and abstract availability: ~200 per sizeable arm, all hits for arms under 200, plus a weighted random sample of 500–1,000 from the union for overall precision at true corpus frequencies. Measure marginal precision — labels among rows a given arm adds after the stronger arms are removed.

Threshold: ≥50% confirmed-eligible-or-appropriately-ambiguous for the core, 95% lower bound above 40%, no arm below ~25% unless it demonstrably carries unique recall for an underserved field.

V3 — rule C, and the cheap tier itself

200–300 labels per major arm. Admission precision of 5–15% is fine for a bounded cheap arm. But the cheap tier is discard-only, so validate it separately on ≥500 human-labelled rule-C rows enriched for hard positives, and require ≤1% confirmed-positive loss with the upper bound below 2% before its discards go live.

V4 — recall, three estimators, none sufficient alone

  1. Known-FLoRA capture (regression test). Map the ~2,978 curated doi_r into the pool and report pool-inclusion recall, B-recall given pool, B∨C-recall given pool, and end-to-end recall including Stage 1 — stratified by year, field, language, abstract availability, and how the record entered FLoRA. Target ≥95% of held-out FLoRA records present in the pool. This is a regression test, not a population estimate: FLoRA is enriched for recent, English, explicitly-titled psychology replications, which is precisely what a lexical whitelist finds easily.
  2. Stratified prevalence sampling of pending (the real estimator). Build strata from signals not identical to the whitelist — revisit/re-estimate/verify vocabulary, robustness language, replication-oriented journals and collections, CS artifact and CODECHECK signals, non-English fields, no-abstract rows, citation links to known originals, and a uniform random stratum. Label 500–1,000 per enriched stratum plus as large a uniform sample as affordable. Estimate missed positives per stratum with inverse-probability weights; recall is estimated B∨C eligible over that plus the weighted pending estimate. Report intervals and stratum-level figures; never pool enriched samples unweighted.
  3. Multi-list capture–recapture (sensitivity only). Three or more lists (FLoRA, replication registries, CODECHECK collections, journal replication collections, citation searches, the new book), log-linear models with source interactions, stratified by field / year / language / genre. Equal catchability and source independence are both badly violated here — FLoRA and lexical rules share their blind spots, and curated projects feed FLoRA directly. Report a range across plausible models, and treat the unseen-population estimate as a diagnostic that the whitelist and FLoRA fail in the same places, not as ground truth.

Acceptance

Adopt when: every live discard subarm clears its own threshold; B fits the expensive budget at its marginal precision; B∨C captures ≥95% of held-out FLoRA records present in the pool; weighted pending audits show no large missed population, with stated uncertainty; no-abstract recall is reported separately and has a funded policy; Stage 1 and Stage 2 consume one canonical vocabulary; and pending is sampled in every release.
