The Stage-2 Rule Book, Reviewed

All 20 filter specs as they stand after the 2026-08-04 evidence reset — each with its full JSON, its current state, and what is wrong with it. Companion to current_state_v4.html, which covers the pipeline as a whole.

The short version. The individual rules are mostly sound; the book is not. Three findings dominate everything below. (1) The pipeline runs two rule evaluators over two corpora, and the second one — keyword_verdict() in Stage 1 — now gates a corpus Stage 2 does not read; §2.1, which answers your search-vs-filter question and corrects a claim I got wrong in the first draft. (2) exclusion-rescue is live and now actively harms rows: it outranks the only admission route and demotes phrase+cite papers from the expensive screen to the cheap tier, rescuing them from rules that no longer discard — §2.2. (3) The phrase alternation is written four times and the exclusion patterns twice, by hand, with the specs themselves documenting the manual sync obligation — §2.3. Beyond those: several descriptions are commit logs rather than policy, two rules exist only because text isn't Unicode-normalised, and two more differ only by an audit obligation.

Superseded in part — read this first. A parallel design pass produced rulebook_v2.html, which proposes inverting Stage 2 from a blacklist (“stem unless excluded”) to a whitelist (“nothing screened unless admitted”), collapsing 20 specs to 6. Its evidence is stronger than anything in the current book: a greedy set cover finds 6 phrase arms carry 92% of positives while the remaining 30 arms buy 1% (28 papers), and bare replication of fires on 82% of screened negatives — so every 500s exclusion rule is a patch on one over-broad admission arm.

If that lands, three findings below are resolved by deletion rather than repair: §2.2's exclusion-rescue demotion bug (the whole 600s rescue band becomes a precedence number), §2.3's four-way phrase duplication (three copies go), and the nfd-stems and RE2-proximity problems. §2.1 is not superseded — it is v2's stated prerequisite, and the per-rule critiques below remain the record of why the current book needs replacing. Read this page as the diagnosis and v2 as the proposed cure.

1 · How to read a rule

Each spec is a JSON object with five decision-bearing fields. Nothing else in the file affects behaviour.

FieldWhat it does
matchThe predicate: text_regex, title_regex, doi_regex, doi_prefix, fields, composed with any_of / all_of / none_of. pyre_regex is a loader-only escape hatch holding the original Python pattern where a lookaround is needed — the decomposition beside it is what RE2 (and so pyarrow) actually evaluates.
pileWhere a claimed row goes: discard (the only rule-terminal state), screen_expensive, screen_cheap, needs_human. pending is engine-assigned and illegal in a spec.
precedenceHigher wins. Multi-match is normal; the pile resolves once. Bands: 900s structural discards · 600s rescues · 500s vocabulary exclusions · 300s expensive routes · 200s cheap routes.
shadowThe draft switch. A shadow rule is still evaluated and its matches are still recorded in evaluations, but it never claims a pile and never affects a row's status. It also bypasses the evidence gate entirely, which is what makes it the honest home for an unmeasured rule.
measuredWhy the rule is allowed to discard. Levels human, downstream, trusted, llm:<model> permit an autonomous discard; heuristic does not. validate_spec() rejects a non-shadow discard whose evidence is all heuristic or absent — and that is the only evidence check in the engine.

The single most important asymmetry, and the best idea in the design: rules route and discard; only LLM tiers admit. A rule that cannot admit cannot do much harm, which is why the routing rules below get a light touch and the discards get a hard one.

2 · The three structural problems

2.1 · Two rule sets, two corpora — and the second gates nothing

You asked what keyword_verdict() does and whether Stage 1 should be filtering at all. It matters here because it determines whether today's reverts mean anything.

keyword_verdict(title, abstract, year) in filter/phrase_detection.py returns one of three verdicts:

  • positive — a precise replication phrase matched, survived the exclusion patterns and the guards.
  • ambiguous — worth a look: either a replication stem in the title with no precise phrase, or a phrase an exclusion killed while a specific author-year cite is present (the #44 readmission).
  • negative — nothing, or an exclusion with nothing to rescue.

It checks exclusions first, and they dominate: “Origins of DNA replication” is refused on the biological pattern before its title stem is considered. It loads those exclusion patterns out of the Stage-2 spec bundle (the seven ids in _EXCLUSION_SPEC_IDS), which is what CLAUDE.md means by “one keyword decision, shared”.

search/snapshot_scan.py calls it as the second admission step (named "Stage B" in the code at the time of writing; that letter naming is being removed). The search gate is a vectorised token gate over the raw inverted-index JSON (a cheap recall query — the only thing that can run over 510,372,821 snapshot rows), yielding roughly 5 million survivors. Every one of those is then passed to the keyword step, which runs keyword_verdict and admits on positive or ambiguous.

Correction to an earlier draft of this page. I first wrote that a keyword negative never reaches Stage 2, making today's shadow flips cosmetic. That is wrong. In _scan_batch(), _pool_record() is appended for every search-gate survivor, unconditionally, before _row_if_admitted() is consulted. The survivor pool therefore holds all ~5M search-gate rows regardless of the keyword verdict — and the pool is what Stage 2 reads (SNAPSHOT_POOL_DIR, the default --pool for every engine subcommand). Shadow works properly: the four drafted exclusions really do stop affecting which papers the pipeline can reach. Today's reverts are real.

But that leaves a stranger problem, and it is the one your “two rule sets” instinct is pointing at.

The keyword verdict gates a corpus nothing downstream reads. What keyword_verdict's verdict actually decides is admission into data/candidates.csv — the Stage-1 artifact of the API-harvest era. Stage 2 reads the pool, not candidates.csv; grep filter/ and extract/ and the only hits are stale comments. So the pipeline currently maintains two corpora (~5M pooled search-gate survivors, and the smaller keyword-admitted CSV) and two rule evaluators (the engine over the spec bundle, and keyword_verdict over Python constants), where the second evaluator's exclusions no longer gate anything that reaches Stage 3.

Which makes CLAUDE.md's “one keyword decision, shared by Stage 1 and Stage 2” false as stated: Stage 2 never calls keyword_verdict at all. The sharing runs the other way and only partly — the seven exclusion specs are loaded out of the bundle into Python for the search gate and keyword step to use. The inclusion phrases, the guards and the stem list are duplicated by hand (§2.3).

Where the boundary should sit

Your rule — Stage 1 searches, Stage 2 filters, one rule set, with the OpenAlex search terms as the allowed exception — is right, and it is closer to done than it looks:

  • The search gate stays in Stage 1. It is the search query: a token/stem gate plus concept membership, the only affordable predicate at 510M rows. This is exactly the carve-out you'd allow, and the stem list is the “OA search terms”.
  • The keyword verdict's gating role should go. Not because it hides rows from Stage 2 (it doesn't), but because it is a second hand-maintained evaluator whose only remaining job is gating a legacy corpus. Retire candidates.csv as an admission-gated artifact, keep the pool as the single corpus, and let the verdict be recorded on the pool row as metadata (it is nearly free — the row already carries hit_token_title/hit_token_abstract/hit_concept) rather than deciding anything.
  • Terminal rules are legitimate — and already exist in the right place. Your instinct that cheap structural rules (work type, deposit DOI prefixes) should settle a row once and not be revisited is exactly what the 900s precedence band does inside the engine: highest precedence, discard, resolved before anything else looks at the row. The need is met; what is not right is that those same mappings are also duplicated as _NON_ARTICLE_TYPES, _DATA_REPOSITORY_PREFIXES and _NON_ARTICLE_DOI_RE in shared/utils.py. One bundle, one evaluator, one band — not a second copy in Python.

The payoff for collapsing to one evaluator is concrete: the entire pyre_regex-vs-RE2 divergence class disappears, and that class is precisely why biological-of and data-availability are stuck in draft. Two rules get unblocked by deleting a code path rather than by running an audit.

2.2 · exclusion-rescue now demotes the rows it was meant to save

The rescue sits at precedence 650 and routes to screen_cheap. The only admission route, phrase-with-cite, sits at 350 and routes to screen_expensive. 650 outranks 350.

That was fine while the 500s exclusions were live: a row matching an exclusion plus a phrase plus a cite was being saved from a terminal discard, and the cheap tier was a promotion. It is not fine now. Four of the seven patterns the rescue copies are shadow and discard nothing, so for those rows the rescue saves them from no danger and takes away the two-voter screen they qualified for on their own evidence.

The rule book already contains this argument, written against itself: title-phrase-rescue's description explains that its data-availability arm was withheld because “a rescue from it could only pull a row DOWN from screen_expensive to screen_cheap”. exclusion-rescue keeps that same data-availability clause. We shadowed the sibling on this reasoning this morning and left the worse offender live.

Two fixes, short and long. Short: restrict the rescue's exclusion clause to the three still-live patterns (biological, structural, editorial-artifact) and extend the test-suite invariant to cover both rescues, not just one. Long: rescues should name the rule ids they rescue from — the engine already records which rules matched each row — instead of re-implementing their regexes. That fixes this bug and §2.3 at the same time.

2.3 · The phrase list is written four times, the exclusions twice

PolicyCopies
The replication phrase alternation (32 arms) phrase-replication.json · phrase-with-cite.json (inlined) · exclusion-rescue.json (inlined) · REPLICATION_PHRASES in filter/phrase_detection.py
The reproduction-anchored patterns (10 arms) phrase-reproduction.json · phrase-with-cite.json · exclusion-rescue.json · _REPRODUCTION_ANCHORED in Python
The seven exclusion patterns their own spec files · re-inlined wholesale in exclusion-rescue.json
The multilingual stem list title-stem.json · REPLICATION_STEM_PATTERN in Python · partially again in nfd-stems.json

Only the exclusion patterns are genuinely single-sourced into Python (the loader pulls them from the bundle — CLAUDE.md's “one edit moves both” is true, but only for those seven). Everything else is hand-synced, and the specs say so: exclusion-rescue's description records that when #147 narrowed three patterns, “the copies here narrowed with them”. That is a maintenance obligation written into a comment, which is the thing declarative configuration is supposed to abolish.

What the format is missing is composition. Something like {"rule_ref": "phrase-replication"} inside a match, resolved at load time, plus {"matched": "technical-verb"} for rescues. Roughly 90 inlined regexes disappear, the four-place edit becomes one place, and the two rescue rules stop being copies of what they rescue from. This is the highest-value refactor in the engine and it is a loader-level change, not a redesign.

2.4 · Smaller things, across the book

  • Descriptions are changelogs. biological-of (~1,900 chars), title-phrase-rescue (~2,000) and exclusion-rescue mix what the rule matches with why arms were added, which issue narrowed it, backend notes and Unicode trivia. A reviewer approving a routing change should not have to read commit history to find the rule. The history is valuable — put it in docs/filter-engine.md or the issue and leave three sentences. (After today's honesty pass several measured rationales are long for the same reason; that is a fair trade for not overclaiming, but they are prose where a structured field would do.)
  • structural.json's description is a malformed fragment — two sentences fused without punctuation, in a file where everything else writes an essay.
  • Two rules differ only by an audit obligation. non-article-type and dataset-type match the same field the same way at 945 / 950. Merge once the dataset audit exists.
  • Two rules exist because text isn't normalised. nfd-stems duplicates part of title-stem to cover decomposed Unicode. NFC-normalise at pool build and delete the rule instead of promoting it.
  • One backend decision recorded as two rule problems. biological-of and data-availability are both shadow because RE2 cannot express a proximity lookahead. That is one engineering choice (get a proximity-capable evaluator — or, per §2.1, stop needing two evaluators at all), owned by nobody.
  • vocabulary: null on every discard rule is noise: the field only means anything on a screen pile. Harmless, but it invites the misreading that a discard could name a vocabulary.
  • No rule has held-out validation. filter/spec/holdout.json is reserved in the loader, referenced in three docs, and has never been created — diagnose reports not_constructed for all 20 rules. Freeze one before any promotion, or the first promotion tunes on its own test set.
  • The reverts should cut both ways. Several arms were rejected on figures from the same compromised gold corpus (the BI1 title rescue “costs ~2,093 rows for one gold positive”; BO3 “admits 3 gold negatives for 2 positives”). Removing the claims that licensed discarding without also re-examining the claims that licensed not rescuing leaves a systematically over-strict book.
